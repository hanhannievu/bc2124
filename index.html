Course overview

This lab will usually be taken concurrently with the BC2125 Personality lecture. It expands upon some of the theoretical, methodological, and analytic issues introduced there, and gives you the opportunity to explore topics from within and beyond those covered in the lectures in greater depth, through hands-on experience with research design and data analysis.

The semester is broken into three projects, each illustrating a common methodological and analytic approach in personality psychology. Projects are undertaken in small groups; group members collaborate on design and analysis. Each project culminates in an in-class group presentation and the submission of a brief individual write-up.

Course goals

Through these projects, you will gain experience in:

  • Formulating psychological research questions relating to personality,
  • Analyzing and visualizing data, and
  • Interpreting and communicating your findings.

Course format

This is a lab course that meets once per week. Labs are substantially more interactive and discussion-based than the traditional lecture format, and depend on everyone’s active participation in class discussions and activities as well as group work focused on the projects. Your active participation across the semester will therefore contribute a substantial portion of your grade.

Workload

As a general rule for the amount of time students should expect to commit to classes, the college suggests three hours per week in or outside of class per credit. Since this class is worth 2.5 credits, that corresponds to 7.5 hours per week, split between time in the classroom and time spent completing the associated assignments.

Course requirements and assignments

Attendance and participation (10%)

Active attendance and participation in class discussions and activities are expected. Normally it is departmental policy to remove students who miss more than two lab sessions from the course; however, given ongoing revisions to college-wide health-related policies, exceptions may be made.

If you are feeling unwell, you should not come to class, and should notify me of nonattendance before class if possible. When you are attending, please arrive on time — frequent lateness will impact your participation grade.

If you have questions, thoughts, or ideas you want to share, feel free to do so at any time. Keep within the bounds of polite conversation, obviously. Don’t interrupt or talk over other people, but do feel free to respond to others without having to raise your hand or wait to be called on. Everyone gets the most out of this lab when discussion develops organically and everyone feels free to be part of the conversation when they have something to add.

Being part of the in-person discussion is one obvious way to participate, but it isn’t the only way. Different people have different styles of participation, and the lab is designed to accommodate and encourage different approaches. Your level of engagement with your project partners as you work through your projects is also an important form of participation. You can also participate by coming to office hours.

At a minimum — that is, for a passing grade — I’ll be looking for some form of participation, loosely defined, from you every week. Higher participation grades are earned through regular, enthusiastic, productive participation. Quality matters more than quantity.

Presentations (30%)

Each project culminates in an in-class group presentation, given on the dates listed in the schedule below. All group members are expected to contribute equally to the preparation and delivery, and all group members receive the same group score, except in exceptional circumstances when it is clear that contributions were not equal.

If a group member is unable to present in person on the day of the presentation due to unforeseen circumstances, please notify Prof. Vu. The presentation will be given by the remaining group members, and the absent member may be assigned the group score, assuming equal contributions to the preparation.

Reports (60%)

Each project also requires a brief individual write-up. Written project reports are due one week following the in-class presentation, which is before the next week’s lab session — see the schedule below for dates.

While you will collaborate with group members on the design, analysis, and presentation of research projects, you may not collaborate on the written report. Each group member must write their own individual report.

Course policies

Academic integrity

Cheating, plagiarism, and other violations of the Student Code of Conduct, Honor Code, and Conduct Process will result in academic consequences. As a Barnard student, you are responsible for reviewing the Student Code of Conduct, Honor Code, and Conduct Process. Violations will be reported to the Honor Board.

You may collaborate with group members on the design, analysis, and presentation of research projects, but you may not collaborate on the written report.

Accessibility

I want this course to provide equitable opportunities for learning. If you have a letter from the Center for Accessibility Resources & Disability Services (CARDS) or have other needs that I can accommodate, please speak with me by the end of the second week of the term to ensure I am able to respond in a timely manner. Should your needs change during the semester, I will be happy to work with you and CARDS to adapt accordingly. All discussions will remain confidential, although CARDS may be consulted to discuss appropriate implementation of accommodations.

Academic accommodations and general wellness

It is always important to recognize the different pressures, burdens, and stressors you may be facing, whether personal, emotional, physical, financial, mental, or academic. The faculty and administration recognize this and are prepared to provide assistance to students in need.

I encourage you to seek advice from your advisor, your Dean, CARDS, or Barnard Health & Wellness as needed. Please let me know of any issues you wish to share with me that you feel are impacting your ability to complete the course to the best of your ability. Though it isn’t always easy, it is better to proactively seek help than to let problems build up.

Late submission policy

Written project reports are due one week following the in-class presentation. A grade penalty of 5 points will be applied for each day (or part thereof) that an assignment is late, up to a maximum of 6 days. Work not submitted before the next lab will receive a score of zero.

For example, if your work is A+ quality but is submitted a day and a half late, you will only receive a B+.

This policy is intended to incentivize timely submission while easing the stress of genuine emergencies. When things come up that prevent timely submission you can prioritize accordingly, knowing that a small penalty on one assignment for this lab will not tank your final grade.

Use of electronics

Electronic devices are permitted for notetaking and class-related activities only. Non-class-related use of electronics, such as browsing social media or gaming, is prohibited during class time.

Eating and drinking

Eating and drinking are allowed in class, provided it is not disruptive to others. Please be considerate and clean up after yourself.

Communication guidelines

  • Respectful communication. All class discussions and debates must be civil, considerate, and free of hate speech and discriminatory language. Avoid terms and phrases that can be exclusionary or derogatory. Use gender-neutral terms and respect others’ gender identities and pronouns.
  • Respect different viewpoints. Respectful debate and disagreement are encouraged, but personal attacks and dismissive comments are not tolerated.
  • Active listening. Practice active listening during discussions, showing respect for others by not interrupting and considering their perspective fully before responding.
  • Confidentiality. Respect the confidentiality of personal information shared by classmates during discussions.
  • Professionalism. Maintain professionalism in communications with the instructor and fellow classmates, including emails.
